Formula 1 cameraman · Director of photography

Jean-MichelTibi

Known in the paddock as the Legend, or simply JMT.

Since 1997 he has worked inside the Formula 1 garages with a slow-motion cinema camera, filming the faces behind the visors for the world feed. Millions of people have watched his pictures. Very few know his name.

On the job

One camera, every garage

Tibi is a freelance cameraman and director of photography for Formula One Management. He works in a small department that handles special projects for sponsors, teams, VIP guests and broadcasters, shooting the artistic requests they cannot shoot themselves. Most of his pictures go out on the international feed that every broadcaster takes.

He covers the paddock and the garages with an ARRI Amira cinema camera on a wireless link, and he always shoots in slow motion. The other garage cameramen each cover two neighbouring teams. He is the only one who works with all of them, so he has to know everyone. On race day he learns people by their silhouettes, because once the helmets and masks go on he can no longer see their faces.

He also worked personally for Bernie Ecclestone for 20 years, and is the official cameraman inside the F1 video game after advising Codemasters on its realism. Lando Norris once told him:

“Even when I’m at home, you’re on my TV with a camera pointed at me!”

His Grand Prix Sunday

From HorsPistesF1, 2021
  1. 07:00
    Coffee first

    Preferably with a red Italian team. He says he only becomes sociable after it, “like a bear coming out of hibernation.”

  2. Arrivals
    Drivers into the paddock

    His pictures go to the world feed and to every broadcaster.

  3. Sessions
    Inside the garages

    FP1, FP2, FP3 and qualifying, from among the mechanics.

  4. Grid
    The last minutes

    Concentration, nerves, breathing, the final words between drivers, engineers and mechanics.

  5. Lights out
    Start reactions

    From the small starter’s hut, or from a garage to catch a team’s reaction. “I do what I feel.”

  6. Race
    Pit stops and faces

    The stops, and the garages’ reactions to overtakes, crashes, yellow and red flags.

  7. Flag
    Chequered flag to champagne

    Winning car, parc fermé, podium and champagne, always in slow motion, in close-up when he can.

  8. Race +2 h
    Last shot, then pack-down

    The winning team with its trophies and drivers. Then the kit goes back in the cases.

Camera report

From Paris courier to the F1 paddock

Photography came first. Racing came through a chain of chance meetings, which is how he describes his whole career: “encounters and a lot of luck.”

YearWhereWhat happened
Early 1980sParis

Wants to be a photographer but has no technique yet. Works as a motorbike courier. After he runs eighteen red lights and gets away from the police, an officer asks to meet him and suggests he apply to become a police motorcyclist.

1985Château de Vincennes

Military service in the French Air Force’s historical service (SHAA). A year of free training in framing, lenses, aperture, depth of field, film stocks and black-and-white printing. Forty years later: “I’m still learning.”

Late 1980sParis

Two or three years as a fashion photographer’s assistant. Then video: weddings, private events and conventions.

1992Formula 1

His first Grands Prix, filming for Shell, a McLaren sponsor, alongside Ron Dennis and Ayrton Senna.

1994Motorsport TV

Meets TV producer Jean-Luc Roy through a friend and becomes a car and motorbike racing cameraman.

1997Formula One Management

Sends his CV to Formula 1 and is hired for three Grands Prix. He has worked for F1 ever since, through the Ecclestone years and the Liberty Media era.

20 yearsBernie Ecclestone

Serves as Ecclestone’s personal cameraman. At their first meeting he contradicts “Mr E”, which Brémond calls the start of their mutual respect.

2019, 2021Monaco

Wins the Sportel Award for best slow motion in Formula 1, twice.

2023City Éditions

Publishes his memoir 30 ans de F1 : Mes histoires secrètes, written with Axel Brémond.

2025Worldwide

Starts his 34th season in Formula 1.

2026Tokyo

The documentary The Legend Behind The Lens comes out in January and is named a finalist at the International Auto Film Festa in April.

Two last pictures

Jules and Anthoine

He filmed the last interview of one young French driver and the last laps of another. He speaks about both in every long interview he gives.

Suzuka · Japanese Grand Prix · 2014

Jules Bianchi

Working for Canal+, the crew asks Bianchi on the grid whether the wet track is raceable. “It’s tricky, but it’ll be fine,” he answers. Laps later he leaves the track where Adrian Sutil has gone off and hits the recovery vehicle. Tibi phones Nicolas Todt in tears and goes to the hospital after the race. Months later he is at the funeral in Nice. A week before Suzuka, the two had spent the night after the Singapore Grand Prix celebrating together.

Spa-Francorchamps · Formula 2 · 2019

Anthoine Hubert

He is following Hubert for a Formula 2 documentary. From the first corner, La Source, he films the formation lap and the start: Hubert’s last two passes. Then the race is stopped and the circuit falls quiet. “Complete silence. It was deafening.” His son was at the circuit that weekend, the only time Tibi brought family to a race, and saw him cry for the only time.

“It’s like a scar on my heart. There should not be any dead or injured people in our world.”

The memoir

Thirty years of secret stories

Jean-Michel Tibi 30 ans de F1Mes histoires secrètes City Éditions · 2023

Typographic stand-in, not the real cover

30 ans de F1 : Mes histoires secrètes

His memoir (in French; the title means “30 Years of F1: My Secret Stories”) collects the stories he could never fit into an interview: his first meeting with Bernie Ecclestone, his dealings with Bernard Tapie, a murder case from his A1GP years, Schumacher, Senna, and the paddock seen from behind the camera.

The book grew out of a 2021 portrait on Axel Brémond’s blog HorsPistesF1. After Tibi posted a tribute to Bernard Tapie, Brémond suggested a book. Tibi’s reply: “If you have a publisher, we’ll do it!” They wrote it over five or six months, with Tibi sending memories by message as they came back to him. The first outline was sketched in a brasserie near the Gare de Lyon.
